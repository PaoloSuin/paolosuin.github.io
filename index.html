Research

From galactic environment to individual stars.

I am interested in the physical links that survive—or disappear—as star formation moves from large scales to small ones.

01

Feedback across scales

How do massive stars regulate the clouds that form them?

Radiation, H II regions and protostellar outflows reshape gas morphology, efficiencies and the star-formation law. I study when feedback suppresses star formation, when it redistributes it, and which signatures survive observational biases.

Radiation-MHDH II regionsJets
02

Structure + magnetic fields

What sets the architecture of star-forming gas?

I follow the evolution of filamentary networks and dense structures to understand how gravity, turbulence, magnetic fields and feedback compete to organize molecular clouds.

FilamentsMagnetic fieldsTopology
03

Cloud → clump populations

How much does large-scale environment matter?

Using kpc-scale simulations, I extract self-consistent populations of star-forming structures and test which cloud-scale conditions propagate into clump properties and star-formation rates.

kpc simulationsClumpsScaling laws
04

Simulations ↔ observations

Make theory observable.

I build analysis pipelines and synthetic observations designed to compare simulations and surveys on equal footing—from cloud statistics to ALMA-scale fragmentation.

Selected publications

A few papers that define the thread.

Two magnetized cloud simulations reveal distinct pathways for filament evolution before feedback, followed by strong restructuring once H II regions expand through the cloud.

arXiv ↗

Radiative feedback changes the star-formation relation primarily through its impact on cloud structure, while protostellar jets strongly reduce the star-formation rate. Mimicking observational limitations also reconciles part of the simulation–observation discrepancy.

arXiv ↗A&A ↗

Coupled N-body and hydrodynamical experiments show that the turbulent gas surrounding a young cluster can accelerate structural evolution and produce dynamics that differ substantially from isolated cluster models.
